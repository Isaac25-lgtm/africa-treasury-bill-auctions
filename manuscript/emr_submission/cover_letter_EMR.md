**Submission: "When Treasury Bill Auctions Miss Their Targets: Bid Volumes, Allotments and Maturity Shifts in Uganda, Kenya and Ghana"**

We submit the above manuscript for consideration as a research article in *Emerging Markets Review*.

Debt managers and market commentators in frontier markets read Treasury bill auctions through two headline numbers: total accepted against total offered, and the published bid-to-cover ratio. Neither shows whether a shortfall happened because bids were missing or because bids were not accepted. Neither shows whether an auction that met its total changed the maturity of new debt. Using 1,210 auction records from the official notices of the central banks of Uganda, Kenya and Ghana, we split every shortfall exactly into these two parts and compare the accepted maturity mix with the offered mix.

The three markets differ sharply. Uganda's shortfalls all occurred with bids at least equal to the offer, while 81% of Kenya's and 75% of Ghana's shortfall volume reflects insufficient bids. Totals hide maturity-level misses in most filled auctions. Filled auctions lengthened the maturity of new issuance in Uganda and shortened it in Kenya. Uganda's published bid-to-cover ratio also changed its denominator over time. A release-aware forecasting test finds that public pre-auction information does not support an operational warning rule, and we report this negative result.

We believe the paper fits the journal's stated preference for comparative studies with regional perspective that take institutional as well as financial perspectives. It combines a three-country comparison, institutional detail drawn from the notices and policy-relevant disclosure recommendations. The full data pipeline, source hashes and a correction ledger are public, so every number can be traced to an official notice.

The closest related work is an IMF staff chapter on Uganda's yield curve (Qu, 2026), which documents post-pandemic divergence between sold and offered amounts in monthly aggregates. We cite it and state clearly what we add: the auction- and maturity-level decomposition, the maturity comparison, the cross-country evidence and the ratio audit.
